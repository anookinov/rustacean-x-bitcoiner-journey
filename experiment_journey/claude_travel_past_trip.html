Rustacean X Bitcoiner Journey

Part 1: Reading the Past Travel Plan

Before Claude can plan a new trip, it needs to understand the existing one. A past trip captured in a Google Sheet is a dense source of real-world data: actual costs versus estimates, timing that worked, logistics that failed, and the sequence of decisions that shaped the experience. This part covers how to prepare that data, bring it into Claude, and extract a research foundation Claude will carry through the rest of the planning process.

In this guide, you will:

  • Structure your existing Google Sheet so Claude can parse it accurately
  • Paste the past trip data into Claude and confirm it was read correctly
  • Run a structured analysis to extract logistics, cost lessons, and booking timing
  • Ask Claude to flag how the itinerary would change for a different traveler profile

Pre-requisites

Preparation

  • Claude Pro subscription (required for long-context pasting)
  • A Google Sheet documenting a past trip — the examples here use a 14-day Vancouver-to-Alaska trip with a 7-day Discovery Princess cruise
  • A Travel Planning Project configured in Claude Desktop (see Claude Desktop: AI Travel Planning Partner)

Step 1: Export the Past Trip Data

  1. Open your Google Sheet with the past trip data

  2. For each tab, copy the cells directly (do not export as file — paste works better):

    • Select all rows including the header row
    • Copy with Cmd+C
    • You will paste each tab as a labeled block in Step 2

  3. If you prefer file export, use File → Download → Comma-separated values (.csv) for each tab and name each file clearly

💡 Paste beats upload for spreadsheet data: Pasting copied cells directly into Claude gives it a cleaner signal than uploading a CSV file. Spreadsheet exports often include invisible characters that confuse column alignment. Copy from the sheet and paste inside the chat.

Step 2: Load the Data into Claude

  1. Open Claude Desktop and start a new conversation inside your Travel Planning Project

  2. Send one message with all four tabs pasted in, labeled by section:

    I am planning a new trip and want to use an existing past trip as a
baseline for research and comparison. Here is the complete data from
that trip, exported from Google Sheets.

Please read all four sections carefully before I ask you any questions.
Confirm once you have processed all four tabs.

--- TAB: TRIP OVERVIEW ---
[paste Tab 1 content here]

--- TAB: DAILY ITINERARY ---
[paste Tab 2 content here]

--- TAB: CRUISE SHORE EXCURSIONS ---
[paste Tab 3 content here]

--- TAB: BUDGET SUMMARY ---
[paste Tab 4 content here]

  3. Wait for Claude to confirm it has read all four sections before continuing

Step 3: Run the Trip Analysis

Once Claude confirms it has the data, ask for a structured analysis:

  1. Send this prompt:

    Based on the past trip data, produce a Trip Analysis covering:

1. Logistics summary
   - Total duration and rough arc of the trip (pre-cruise, cruise, post-cruise)
   - Cruise ship, route, embarkation port, duration
   - Number of hotel nights and city (pre and post)

2. Cost breakdown
   - Total actual cost and cost per person per day
   - Categories that ran over budget and by how much
   - Top 3 largest line items
   - Hidden or underestimated costs (flag these explicitly)

3. What worked well
   - Activities rated 9/10 or above with a one-sentence reason
   - Booking decisions that saved time or money
   - Logistics that made the trip smoother

4. What to do differently
   - Anything rated below 8/10 with a note on why
   - Budget categories significantly underestimated
   - Timing or booking mistakes worth avoiding

5. Must-book-early items
   - Excursions or activities that required advance booking
   - Anything weather-dependent with a backup recommendation
   - Anything that was close to selling out or required early action

  2. Save Claude’s output — you will reference the Trip Analysis in Part 2 when profiling the new traveler

Step 4: Ask About Adaptability

Before closing this conversation, probe how the itinerary would change for a different traveler:

  1. Ask about booking timing:

    Based on the past trip notes, build a booking timeline.
List each trip component (cruise, flights, hotels, excursions) with:
- How far in advance to book
- Why (sold out quickly, price increases closer to date, etc.)
- Any component that was booked too late or cut it close

  2. Ask about seasonal flexibility:

    The past trip ran in June. What aspects of this itinerary are
season-specific — where a different month would significantly change
the experience, cost, or availability? Include:
- Wildlife viewing (whales, bears) by month
- Weather risk at each Alaskan port
- Cruise pricing patterns (peak vs. shoulder season)
- Glacier visibility at Tracy Arm Fjord

  3. Ask about traveler profile flexibility:

    How adaptable is this itinerary for travelers with different needs?
Flag any activities or logistics that would change significantly for:
- A traveler with limited mobility (knee replacement, prefers paved surfaces)
- A solo traveler on a tighter budget
- A family with children under 12
- A traveler who has never been on a cruise before

For each profile, name the 2–3 items that need the most adjustment.

💡 Keep this conversation open: Do not close it before Part 2. Claude retains the full trip data and analysis within the same session. If you need to continue later, start a new conversation with: “I analyzed a 14-day Vancouver–Alaska Princess cruise trip in a previous session. Here is the Trip Analysis we produced: [paste the analysis output].”

Troubleshooting

Claude says the pasted data is too long

  • Split the paste into two messages: Trip Overview and Daily Itinerary first, then Shore Excursions and Budget Summary second
  • Tell Claude: “I will send the data in two parts. Confirm when you are ready for Part 2.”

Claude misreads the column structure or confuses values

  • Check that your paste includes the header row — Claude infers column meaning from headers
  • If the table structure broke during paste, re-copy from Google Sheets using Edit → Copy (not Export) and paste into a plain text editor first to verify it looks correct

The trip notes are sparse and Claude cannot extract much

  • Add context manually before the analysis prompt: “The notes column is minimal. Here is additional context for specific days: [add your recollections about what worked or did not]”
  • Claude works well with informal memory notes alongside structured data
