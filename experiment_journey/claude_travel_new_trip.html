Part 3: Building the New Trip Plan & Creating the Google Sheet

With a baseline trip analyzed, a detailed traveler profile built, and current conditions researched, Claude now has everything it needs to generate a complete customized itinerary. This part covers how to ask Claude to synthesize all of that into a tailored trip plan, how to format it for direct paste into Google Sheets, and how to iterate until the plan fits the traveler precisely.

In this guide, you will:

  • Ask Claude to synthesize the baseline analysis, traveler profile, and research into a full itinerary
  • Iterate on specific sections — excursions, budget, pace — without rebuilding from scratch
  • Generate four Google Sheet tabs in CSV format ready for copy-paste
  • Populate a new Google Sheet and run a final review with Claude

Pre-requisites

Preparation

  • The Trip Analysis from Part 1 (in the active conversation or saved to paste back in)
  • The Conditions Comparison Summary from Part 2
  • The traveler profile from Part 2
  • A blank Google Sheet ready to receive the output — name it something like Alaska 2025 — Margaret

References

  • Claude.ai
  • Google Sheets

Step 1: Generate the New Itinerary

  1. Continue in the same conversation from Parts 1 and 2, or open a new conversation in your Travel Planning Project and paste the Trip Analysis and Conditions Comparison Summary at the top

  2. Send the synthesis prompt:

    Using everything we have established — the past trip analysis, Margaret's
traveler profile, and the research on current conditions — please build a
complete 14-day itinerary for her new trip.

REQUIREMENTS:
- Start and end in Vancouver (Canada Place)
- Include a 7-day Princess Cruise on the Inside Passage route
- Adapt all excursions for Margaret's mobility (max 2km paved walking,
  no steep terrain, no stairs-heavy activities)
- Stay within CAD $9,000–$11,000 total (solo, single-occupancy)
- Travel window: August 14–28, 2025

FORMAT — produce four tables for Google Sheets tabs:

TABLE 1: Daily Itinerary
Day | Date | Location | Activity / Plan | Estimated Cost CAD | Notes

TABLE 2: Cruise Shore Excursions
Port | Excursion | Accessibility | Book Through | Est. Cost CAD | Book By | Notes

TABLE 3: Accommodation
Dates | Property | Location | Nightly Rate CAD | Total CAD | Notes

TABLE 4: Budget Estimate
Category | Estimated CAD | Notes

After the tables, add a Key Adaptations section listing the 5 most
significant changes made relative to the 2023 baseline trip, and
the reason each was made for Margaret specifically.

  3. Review each table before iterating — flag anything that conflicts with what you know about the traveler’s constraints or the researched prices

Step 2: Iterate on Specific Sections

Rarely does the first plan need no changes. Adjust by section rather than regenerating the entire itinerary:

  1. Adjust an excursion that does not fit the mobility profile:

    The Mendenhall Glacier hike involves 1.5km of uneven glacier moraine
terrain — not suitable for Margaret's knee. She still wants a Juneau
glacier experience.

Suggest two accessible Juneau alternatives that:
- Offer comparable glacier or wildlife viewing
- Require less than 1km of walking on paved or stable surfaces
- Are bookable through Princess or a local operator
- Fall within a similar cost range (CAD $100–$200)

For each: name, brief description, why it fits Margaret, and how
to book it.

  2. Bring the budget into range if the estimate is over target:

    The current estimate puts the trip at CAD $12,100 — above Margaret's
$11,000 ceiling. Find CAD $1,100 in savings without significantly
reducing the experience.

For each saving option: the amount saved, the experience trade-off,
and your recommendation on whether it is worth it for this traveler.
Consider:
- Interior vs. balcony cabin (weigh the accessibility argument for balcony)
- One fewer pre-cruise night
- Excursion substitutions
- Meal or onboard spending adjustments

  3. Adjust the pace for a first-time cruiser:

    Margaret has never cruised before and may find a fully scheduled
port day tiring. Identify the one port day where I should leave the
afternoon unscheduled — no bookings, no transfers — so she can
rest or explore independently at her own pace.

Recommend which port and explain why that day is the best to keep open.

  4. Add weather contingency plans:

    What are the most weather-dependent activities in this itinerary?
For each one, provide:
- The weather risk in August at that port
- A backup activity that is accessible for Margaret and does not
  require advance booking
- Whether the primary excursion operator offers a rain-day refund
  or reschedule

Step 3: Generate the Final Google Sheets Output

Once the itinerary is finalized through iteration, ask Claude to produce a clean, paste-ready output:

  1. Request the full CSV output:

    Produce the final itinerary as four separate CSV blocks, one per
Google Sheet tab. Use commas as delimiters. Wrap any field that
contains a comma in double quotes.

Label each block clearly before the CSV rows:

=== TAB: Daily Itinerary ===
[CSV rows]

=== TAB: Cruise Shore Excursions ===
[CSV rows]

=== TAB: Accommodation ===
[CSV rows]

=== TAB: Budget Estimate ===
[CSV rows]

  2. Add a fifth tab summarizing the trip for the traveler:

    Add a fifth block for a Trip Summary tab. This tab is for Margaret to
read directly — written from her perspective, not the planner's.

Include:
- The 5 things she most needs to know before departure
- A day-by-day summary in 2–4 words per day (e.g., "Arrive Vancouver",
  "Stanley Park morning", "Board Discovery Princess")
- The 3 excursions that must be confirmed as booked before she leaves home
- What to pack that is specific to this itinerary (not a generic list)
- Emergency contacts and booking reference summary (leave placeholders
  for actual confirmation numbers)

=== TAB: Trip Summary ===
[CSV rows]

Step 4: Populate the Google Sheet

  1. Open Google Sheets at sheets.google.com and create a new blank spreadsheet

  2. Name it Alaska 2025 — Margaret (or your traveler’s name)

  3. For each CSV block from Claude:

    1. Click the + tab button at the bottom to add a new sheet tab

    2. Name the tab to match the block label — Daily Itinerary, Cruise Shore Excursions, Accommodation, Budget Estimate, Trip Summary

    3. Click cell A1 in the new tab

    4. Copy the CSV rows from Claude (include the header row)

    5. Paste using Edit → Paste special → Paste as values only (or Cmd+Shift+V on Mac)

    6. Select all populated columns and choose Format → Column → Fit to data to auto-resize

  4. On the Budget Estimate tab, add a totals row:

    • Click the first empty cell below the last budget category in column B
    • Enter =SUM(B2:B[last row number]) to get a live running total
    • As you adjust any cost estimate, this cell updates automatically

  5. On the Daily Itinerary tab, highlight high-cost days:

    • Select the Estimated Cost CAD column
    • Go to Format → Conditional formatting
    • Add a rule: cell value greater than 300 → fill color yellow
    • This flags days worth reviewing if the budget needs trimming

💡 Google Sheets import as an alternative: For long outputs with special characters, save Claude’s CSV as a .csv file using TextEdit or Notepad, then use File → Import in Google Sheets. This handles edge cases more reliably than paste.

Step 5: Final Review with Claude

Before closing the planning session, run one last check:

  1. Ask Claude to review the completed plan for gaps:

    Here is the final trip summary for Margaret:
[paste the Trip Summary tab content]

Please review and identify:
1. Any logistical gaps — transitions that are too tight, bookings that
   depend on each other without a buffer
2. Anything inconsistent with Margaret's profile: mobility, solo travel,
   first-time cruiser, or budget
3. Booking checklist items that must happen immediately vs. closer to
   departure
4. One thing we have probably not thought of for a solo senior traveler
   on a first Alaska cruise

  2. Ask for a one-page printable trip brief for the traveler:

    Generate a one-page trip brief Margaret can print and carry.
Write it for her to read directly — not a planning document.

Include:
- Trip at a glance: dates, ship name, departure port
- Day-by-day summary (2–4 words per day)
- The 3 most important bookings to have confirmed before departure
- 5 things specific to Alaska in August she should know before she goes
- What to pack that most guides miss for this itinerary
- One sentence for each port: what is worth seeing if nothing else

Format for print: clear headers, no tables, readable at 12pt.

Troubleshooting

CSV output has formatting errors when pasted into Google Sheets

  • Use Paste special → Values only (Cmd+Shift+V) to prevent Google Sheets from interpreting the content as HTML
  • If a cell bleeds into the next column, the source field has an unquoted comma — ask Claude: “Field [X] has a formatting issue in the CSV. Re-generate that tab with all comma-containing fields wrapped in double quotes.”

The budget estimate is significantly higher or lower than expected

  • Ask Claude to justify each line: “Walk me through the Accommodation estimate — which property, how many nights, and what rate per night are you assuming?”
  • Paste in actual rates you have verified and ask: “Update the Budget Estimate tab using these confirmed prices: [list]. Recalculate the total.”

The new itinerary looks too similar to the baseline

  • Ask for explicit differentiation: “This plan is quite similar to the 2023 baseline. Propose 5 meaningful changes that make this feel like a genuinely different trip for Margaret — not just swaps for mobility, but things that would make it memorable for a first-time solo cruiser.”

Claude cannot find the specific sailing dates or pricing

  • This is likely a knowledge cutoff issue. Ask: “Give me the exact URL and search path on Princess Cruises’ website to find Alaska August 2025 sailings and solo pricing. I will look it up and paste the results.”
  • Once you paste the current data, Claude can incorporate it immediately into the plan
